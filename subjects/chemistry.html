1. Atomic Structure

An atom consists of protons (+), neutrons (neutral) in the nucleus, and electrons (−) in shells.

Mass Number (A) = Protons + Neutrons Atomic Number (Z) = Protons = Electrons (in neutral atom) Electron Configuration: 2, 8, 8, 18, ...
Example: Write the electron configuration of Calcium (Z = 20).

Step 1 — Total electrons = 20
Step 2 — Fill shells: K=2, L=8, M=8, N=2
✅ Configuration: 2, 8, 8, 2

2. Chemical Bonding

Ionic bond — transfer of electrons (metal + non-metal). Example: NaCl
Covalent bond — sharing of electrons. Example: H₂O
Metallic bond — sea of delocalised electrons.

Example: Explain bond in MgCl₂.

Step 1 — Mg has 2 outer electrons; Cl has 7.
Step 2 — Mg loses 2 electrons → Mg²⁺
Step 3 — Each Cl gains 1 electron → 2Cl⁻
✅ Ionic bond: Mg²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ → MgCl₂

3. The Mole Concept

1 mole = 6.02 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number) Moles = Mass / Molar Mass Moles = Volume / 22.4 (at STP, gases)
Example: How many moles in 80 g of NaOH? (Na = 23, O = 16, H = 1)

Step 1 — Molar mass of NaOH = 23 + 16 + 1 = 40 g/mol
Step 2 — Moles = 80 / 40 = 2 mol
✅ 2 moles

4. Balancing Chemical Equations

Same number of each atom must appear on both sides of the equation.

Example: Balance H₂ + O₂ → H₂O

Step 1 — Count atoms: LHS H=2, O=2; RHS H=2, O=1
Step 2 — Balance O by placing 2 in front of H₂O: H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
Step 3 — Now LHS H=2, RHS H=4. Place 2 in front of H₂: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
Step 4 — Final check: LHS H=4, O=2; RHS H=4, O=2 ✓
✅ 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

5. Acids, Bases & Salts

Acid + Base → Salt + Water (neutralization)

pH = −log[H⁺] pH < 7 = acidic, pH = 7 neutral, pH > 7 basic
Example (Titration): 25 cm³ of 0.1 M HCl is neutralised by NaOH. If 20 cm³ of NaOH was used, find its concentration.

Step 1 — Equation: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O (1:1 ratio)
Step 2 — Moles of HCl = (25/1000) × 0.1 = 0.0025 mol
Step 3 — Moles of NaOH = 0.0025 mol (1:1)
Step 4 — Concentration = moles/volume = 0.0025 / (20/1000) = 0.125 M
✅ [NaOH] = 0.125 M

6. Redox Reactions

Oxidation = loss of electrons (OIL). Reduction = gain of electrons (RIG).

Example: In Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu, identify oxidised and reduced species.

Step 1 — Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ (loses electrons → oxidised)
Step 2 — Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (gains electrons → reduced)
✅ Zn is oxidised; Cu²⁺ is reduced

7. Organic Chemistry

Compounds of carbon. Major homologous series:

  • Alkanes: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (saturated, e.g. CH₄ methane)
  • Alkenes: CₙH₂ₙ (one double bond, e.g. C₂H₄ ethene)
  • Alkynes: CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ (one triple bond, e.g. C₂H₂ ethyne)
  • Alcohols: CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH (e.g. C₂H₅OH ethanol)
Example: Combustion of methane.

Step 1 — Hydrocarbons burn to give CO₂ + H₂O
Step 2 — CH₄ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O
Step 3 — Balance: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O
✅ CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O
