1. Motion & Kinematics

Kinematics describes how objects move using equations of motion.

v = u + at s = ut + ½at² v² = u² + 2as where u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = acceleration, t = time, s = displacement
Example: A car starts from rest and accelerates at 4 m/s² for 6 seconds. Find (a) final velocity, (b) distance covered.

Step 1 — Identify given: u = 0, a = 4 m/s², t = 6 s
Step 2 (a) — Use v = u + at → v = 0 + (4)(6) = 24 m/s
Step 3 (b) — Use s = ut + ½at² → s = 0 + ½(4)(6²) = ½(4)(36) = 72 m
✅ v = 24 m/s, s = 72 m

2. Forces & Newton's Laws

1st Law: An object stays at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a force.
2nd Law: F = ma
3rd Law: Action and reaction are equal and opposite.

F = ma (Force = mass × acceleration) W = mg (Weight = mass × gravity, g = 10 m/s²)
Example: A 5 kg object is pushed with a force of 20 N. Find its acceleration.

Step 1 — Use Newton's 2nd Law: F = ma → a = F/m
Step 2 — a = 20 / 5 = 4 m/s²
✅ a = 4 m/s²

3. Work, Energy & Power

Work: W = F × d Kinetic Energy: KE = ½mv² Potential Energy: PE = mgh Power: P = W / t or P = Fv
Example: A 2 kg ball moves at 6 m/s. Find its KE.

Step 1 — Formula: KE = ½mv²
Step 2 — KE = ½(2)(6²) = ½(2)(36) = 36 J
✅ KE = 36 Joules

4. Waves & Sound

v = f λ where v = wave speed, f = frequency, λ = wavelength
Example: A wave has frequency 50 Hz and wavelength 4 m. Find its speed.

Step 1 — Use v = fλ
Step 2 — v = 50 × 4 = 200 m/s
✅ v = 200 m/s

5. Electricity

Ohm's Law: V = IR Power: P = VI = I²R = V²/R Q = It (Charge)
Example: A 12 V battery drives a 4 Ω resistor. Find current and power.

Step 1 — Use Ohm's Law: I = V/R = 12 / 4 = 3 A
Step 2 — Power: P = VI = 12 × 3 = 36 W
✅ I = 3 A, P = 36 W

6. Heat & Thermodynamics

Q = mcΔθ (specific heat capacity) Q = ml (latent heat)
Example: Heat needed to raise 2 kg of water from 20°C to 80°C? (c = 4200 J/kg·K)

Step 1 — ΔT = 80 − 20 = 60 K
Step 2 — Q = mcΔT = 2 × 4200 × 60
Step 3 — Q = 504,000 J = 504 kJ
✅ Q = 504,000 J

7. Modern Physics

Photoelectric effect, atomic structure, radioactivity.

E = hf (energy of photon) E = mc² (mass-energy equivalence) Half-life: N = N₀ (½)^(t/T)
Example: A radioactive element has half-life 10 days. After 30 days, what fraction remains?

Step 1 — Number of half-lives: n = 30/10 = 3
Step 2 — Fraction remaining = (½)³ = 1/8
✅ 1/8 of original remains
