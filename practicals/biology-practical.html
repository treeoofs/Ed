DrawingWAEC 2022

P3. Biological Drawing Rules

  • Use a sharp HB pencil only — never colour or shade.
  • Make drawings large (at least half page) and clear.
  • Lines must be single, firm, and continuous — no sketching.
  • Label lines should not cross each other.
  • Labels written in lowercase except proper nouns; pulled out horizontally.
  • State magnification: M = drawing length / actual length.
  • Title written below drawing — underlined.
  • Show only what you observe — do NOT add details from memory.

MicroscopyWAEC 2020

P5. Onion Epidermis under Microscope

Procedure: Peel a thin transparent layer from inside of onion bulb. Place on clean slide. Add 1 drop iodine. Cover with cover slip avoiding bubbles. Observe under low then high power.

Observations:

  • Brick-shaped or rectangular cells arranged in rows.
  • Each cell has a thick cell wall, large central vacuole, nucleus stained yellow-brown by iodine, and cytoplasm.
  • No chloroplasts (storage tissue, not photosynthetic).
Plant PhysiologyWAEC 2019

P6. Investigation of Photosynthesis (Starch test)

  1. Place a destarched leaf in light for 4 hours.
  2. Pluck the leaf; boil in water for 1 minute (to kill cells).
  3. Boil in ethanol (water bath) for 5 minutes — leaf becomes brittle and decolorised.
  4. Wash in warm water to soften.
  5. Spread on white tile; add iodine solution.
  6. Observation: Leaf turns blue-black → starch present → photosynthesis occurred.
EcologyWAEC 2018

P7. Sampling — Quadrat Method

Use: Estimate population of plants/sessile animals in a habitat.

Procedure:

  1. Throw quadrat (1 m²) randomly in habitat — repeat 10 times.
  2. Count organisms in each quadrat. Find mean per m².
  3. Multiply mean by total habitat area.
  4. Example: mean = 8 per m²; field = 200 m² → estimated population = 1600.
