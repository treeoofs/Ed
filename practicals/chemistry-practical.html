Volumetric Analysis WAEC 2024 Quantitative

P1. Acid-Base Titration

Question: A is a solution of HCl containing 7.30 g per dm³. B is 0.100 mol/dm³ of Na₂CO₃. Using methyl orange indicator, titrate B against A. Pipette 25.0 cm³ of B into a conical flask. Record your titre values and calculate the molar concentration of A.

🔬 Apparatus Required

Burette (50 cm³)
Pipette (25 cm³)
Conical flask
Pipette filler
White tile
Methyl orange
Funnel
Wash bottle

📋 Procedure

  1. Rinse the burette with solution A and fill it to the zero mark, ensuring no air bubbles below the tap.
  2. Rinse the pipette with solution B; pipette exactly 25.0 cm³ of B into a clean conical flask.
  3. Add 2-3 drops of methyl orange indicator. Solution turns yellow.
  4. Place flask on white tile under burette. Run A in slowly, swirling continuously.
  5. End point: solution turns from yellow → orange → just pink/red. Record final burette reading.
  6. Repeat at least 3 times to obtain concordant titres (within 0.10 cm³ of each other).

📊 Sample Burette Readings

TitrationRough1st2nd3rd
Final burette reading (cm³)25.2024.9024.8524.95
Initial burette reading (cm³)0.000.000.000.10
Volume of A used (cm³)25.2024.9024.8524.85

Average titre: (24.90 + 24.85 + 24.85)/3 = 24.87 cm³

🧮 Calculations

Step 1: Write balanced equation:

2HCl + Na₂CO₃ → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂ (mole ratio 2 : 1)

Step 2: Calculate moles of Na₂CO₃ in 25.0 cm³ of B:

n(Na₂CO₃) = C × V = 0.100 × (25.0/1000) = 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol

Step 3: Use mole ratio to find moles of HCl:

n(HCl) = 2 × n(Na₂CO₃) = 2 × 2.50 × 10⁻³ = 5.00 × 10⁻³ mol

Step 4: Calculate concentration of HCl (solution A):

C(A) = n / V = (5.00 × 10⁻³) / (24.87/1000) = 0.201 mol/dm³

Step 5: Verify with given data: A contains 7.30 g/dm³

Theoretical: C = 7.30/36.5 = 0.200 mol/dm³ ✓ % error = |0.201 − 0.200|/0.200 × 100 = 0.5% (excellent)

✅ Final Answer: Molar concentration of A ≈ 0.20 mol/dm³

Examiner Tip: Show the burette readings table, calculate the average from concordant titres only (ignore rough), state the equation clearly, and always show units throughout.

P2. Identification of Salt C

Question: C is a salt. Carry out the following tests on C and record your observations and inferences.

📋 Test Results Table (C is FeSO₄·7H₂O — Iron(II) sulphate heptahydrate)

TestObservationInference
(a) Heat C in a dry test tube Green crystals turn white, then brown; colourless droplets condense at cooler part of tube Hydrated salt; Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ on heating; H₂O liberated
(b) Dissolve C in water; add NaOH(aq) dropwise then in excess Dirty green gelatinous precipitate; insoluble in excess; turns brown on standing in air Fe(OH)₂ formed; oxidised to Fe(OH)₃ on exposure to air → confirms Fe²⁺
(c) To fresh solution of C, add NH₃(aq) dropwise then excess Dirty green precipitate; insoluble in excess Confirms Fe²⁺ (Fe(OH)₂ insoluble in excess NH₃)
(d) Acidify with dilute HCl, then add BaCl₂(aq) White precipitate, insoluble in dilute HCl SO₄²⁻ confirmed (BaSO₄ formed)
(e) Add K₃[Fe(CN)₆] (potassium hexacyanoferrate(III)) to C solution Deep blue precipitate (Turnbull's blue) Confirms Fe²⁺

🧪 Conclusion

Salt C = FeSO₄·7H₂O (Iron(II) sulphate heptahydrate, Green Vitriol)

⚖️ Key Equations

Fe²⁺(aq) + 2OH⁻(aq) → Fe(OH)₂(s) ↓ dirty green 4Fe(OH)₂(s) + O₂(g) + 2H₂O → 4Fe(OH)₃(s) ↓ brown Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s) ↓ white

VolumetricWAEC 2021Redox Titration

P4. KMnO₄ vs FeSO₄ Titration

Question: Titrate 0.020 mol/dm³ KMnO₄ against 25.0 cm³ of acidified FeSO₄ solution. Calculate the concentration of FeSO₄.

Equation (ionic):

MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O + 5Fe³⁺ (1:5 ratio)

End point: First permanent pink colour (no indicator needed — KMnO₄ is self-indicating).

Sample average titre: 20.00 cm³

Calculation:

n(KMnO₄) = 0.020 × (20.00/1000) = 4.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol n(Fe²⁺) = 5 × n(KMnO₄) = 5 × 4.00 × 10⁻⁴ = 2.00 × 10⁻³ mol [Fe²⁺] = (2.00 × 10⁻³) / (25.0/1000) = 0.080 mol/dm³

✅ [FeSO₄] = 0.08 mol/dm³

P6. NaOH vs H₂SO₄ Titration

Question: 25.0 cm³ of 0.10 M NaOH neutralized 22.5 cm³ of H₂SO₄. Find the concentration of H₂SO₄.

2NaOH + H₂SO₄ → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O (2:1 ratio) n(NaOH) = 0.10 × 25.0/1000 = 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol n(H₂SO₄) = ½ × n(NaOH) = 1.25 × 10⁻³ mol [H₂SO₄] = (1.25 × 10⁻³) / (22.5/1000) = 0.0556 mol/dm³

✅ [H₂SO₄] ≈ 0.056 mol/dm³

QuantitativeWAEC 2024Solubility

P8. Solubility Determination

Question: 5.0 g of salt G dissolves in 25 g of water at 30°C. Calculate solubility in g per 100 g of water.

Solubility = (mass of solute / mass of water) × 100 = (5.0 / 25) × 100 = 20 g per 100 g of water

✅ Solubility of G at 30°C = 20 g/100 g water

P10. Determination of Empirical Formula

Question: A compound contains 40% C, 6.7% H, 53.3% O. Find empirical formula. (C=12, H=1, O=16)

Element | % | ÷ atomic mass | Mole ratio | ÷ smallest C | 40 | 40/12 = 3.33 | 3.33 | 3.33/3.33 = 1 H | 6.7 | 6.7/1 = 6.7 | 6.7 | 6.7/3.33 ≈ 2 O | 53.3 | 53.3/16 = 3.33 | 3.33 | 3.33/3.33 = 1

✅ Empirical formula = CH₂O (e.g. formaldehyde or glucose unit)
