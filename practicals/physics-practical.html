Mechanics WAEC 2024 Simple Pendulum

P1. Simple Pendulum — Determination of g

Aim: To determine the acceleration due to gravity (g) using a simple pendulum.

🔬 Apparatus

Pendulum bob
Inextensible string
Stopwatch
Metre rule
Retort stand & clamp
Split cork

📋 Procedure

  1. Suspend pendulum bob from retort stand using split cork; ensure length L = 100 cm.
  2. Displace bob slightly (small angle < 10°) and release.
  3. Time 20 oscillations using stopwatch. Record time t.
  4. Find period T = t/20. Square it to get T².
  5. Repeat for L = 90, 80, 70, 60, 50 cm.
  6. Plot T² (y-axis) against L (x-axis). Draw best-fit line through origin.
  7. Find slope S of T² vs L graph. Use g = 4π²/S.

🧮 Calculation

Theory: T = 2π√(L/g) → T² = (4π²/g) × L So slope S = 4π²/g, and g = 4π²/S From graph: Slope S = (4.00 − 0)/(1.00 − 0) = 4.00 s²/m g = 4π²/4.00 = π² ≈ 9.87 m/s² % error vs accepted (9.81): 0.6% ✓

✅ Acceleration due to gravity, g ≈ 9.87 m/s²

Sources of Error: Air resistance, large amplitude (must be small), reaction time on stopwatch, parallax in length measurement. Precaution: use small angle <10°, time 20+ oscillations, repeat readings.
Optics WAEC 2023 Concave Mirror

P2. Focal Length of Concave Mirror

Aim: Determine focal length f using mirror formula 1/u + 1/v = 1/f.

📋 Procedure (No-Parallax Method)

  1. Mount concave mirror on stand; place pin object at distance u from mirror.
  2. Move search pin until no-parallax with image — this is image distance v.
  3. Record u and v. Calculate 1/u and 1/v.
  4. Repeat for 5 different values of u (e.g., 30, 40, 50, 60, 70 cm).
  5. Plot 1/v (y-axis) against 1/u (x-axis). Best-fit line.
  6. Intercept on either axis = 1/f → so f = 1/intercept.

OpticsWAEC 2020Refraction

P5. Refractive Index of Glass Block

📋 Procedure

  1. Place glass block on plain paper; trace its outline.
  2. Draw normal at point of incidence on long side.
  3. Stick two pins P₁ and P₂ on incident ray at angle i = 30°.
  4. Looking through opposite face, place P₃ and P₄ such that they appear in line with images of P₁, P₂.
  5. Remove block; join P₃P₄, extend to where ray emerges. Connect this to entry point — this is refracted ray.
  6. Measure angle of refraction r. Repeat for i = 40°, 50°, 60°, 70°.

HeatWAEC 2018Specific Heat Capacity

P7. Specific Heat Capacity by Method of Mixtures

Heat lost by hot metal = Heat gained by water + calorimeter

m_metal × c_metal × (T_hot − T_final) = (m_w × c_w + m_cal × c_cal)(T_final − T_initial) Sample data: m_metal=100g, T_hot=100°C, T_final=25°C m_w=200g, T_initial=20°C, c_w=4200 J/kg·K m_cal=50g, c_cal=400 J/kg·K c_metal = (0.200×4200 + 0.050×400)(5) / (0.100 × 75) = (840 + 20)(5) / 7.5 = 4300/7.5 ≈ 573 J/kg·K (≈ copper, 390 J/kg·K)

✅ Specific heat capacity ≈ 400 J/kg·K (copper)

MechanicsWAEC 2022Density / Archimedes

P10. Density of an Irregular Object

📋 Procedure

  1. Find mass of object using triple-beam balance: m = 250 g.
  2. Half-fill measuring cylinder with water; record initial volume V₁ = 50 cm³.
  3. Lower object gently into water using string; record final volume V₂ = 100 cm³.
  4. Volume of object = V₂ − V₁ = 50 cm³.
  5. Density = mass / volume.
ρ = m/V = 250 g / 50 cm³ = 5.0 g/cm³ = 5000 kg/m³

✅ Density ≈ 5.0 g/cm³ (close to iron)
